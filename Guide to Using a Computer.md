**Introduction**

Learning how to use a computer doesn't have to be difficult. Whether you're using one for the first time or trying to improve your skills, the most important thing is to start with the basics and practise regularly.

A computer can help you learn, work, communicate, create documents, browse the internet, and do so many other things.

This guide will walk you through the essentials in simple, everyday language.

 **2. How to Turn a Computer On and Off**

It sounds simple, but learning how to start and shut down a computer properly is important.

**To turn it on:**

1. Connect the power supply if necessary.
2. Press the power button.
3. Wait for the computer to start.
4. Enter your password if requested.

**To turn it off:**

1. Save any work you are doing.
2. Click the Start menu (on Windows).
3. Select Power.
4. Click Shut down.
5. Wait for the computer to switch off.

Avoid switching off the power directly while the computer is running.

**5. Understanding Files and Folders**

Think of your computer as a filing cabinet.

- A **file** is an individual document, picture, video, or other item.
- A **folder** is where you keep and organise files.

For example, you could create a folder called *My Documents* and save your assignments, pictures, and personal documents inside it.

Keeping your files organised makes them easier to find.

The internet allows you to access information, communicate with people, watch videos, attend online classes, and much more.

**7. Learning Basic Computer Applications**

Here are some applications worth becoming familiar with:

- **Microsoft Word:** For writing letters, reports, and assignments.
- **Microsoft Excel:** For organising information, calculations, and tables.
- **Microsoft PowerPoint:** For creating presentations.
- **Google Chrome:** For browsing the internet.
- **VLC Media Player:** For playing videos and audio.

You don't need to master everything at once. Start with the applications you need most.

**8. Basic Computer Safety**

Your safety matters just as much as your computer skills.

Always remember to:

- Use strong passwords.
- Avoid downloading files from unknown websites.
- Don't open suspicious email attachments.
- Keep your computer updated.
- Log out when using a shared computer.
- Back up important files.

Being careful online helps protect your information and your device.

**9. Practise What You've Learned**

The best way to become comfortable with a computer is to use it.

Try these simple activities:

1. Create a folder on your desktop called *My Practice*.
2. Open Microsoft Word or another text editor.
3. Type a short paragraph about yourself.
4. Save the document inside your new folder.
5. Copy the document and paste it somewhere else.
6. Open your web browser and search for something you want to learn.

These small activities will help you build confidence.

**Final Thoughts**

Nobody becomes a computer expert in one day. Everyone starts somewhere, and making mistakes is part of learning.

Take your time, explore the different features, ask questions, and practise whenever you can.

The more you use a computer, the more comfortable you'll become. Remember, **you don't have to know everything to get started; you just have to be willing to learn.**
